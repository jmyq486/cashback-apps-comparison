# top cash back: how to find the app that actually pays cash into your bank account, plus the rates, caps and payout rules that decide what you keep

Search "top cash back" and you'll get two different things mixed together. One is TopCashback, the UK-born cashback site that plenty of people assume works everywhere. The other is the general question: which cashback app in Singapore actually returns the most money?

Start with the first one, because it saves you time. TopCashback has no Singapore operation. Searching for topcashback.sg lands you on topcashback.com, the US site, built around US retailers and US payout methods — which is close to useless if your shopping happens on Shopee, Lazada, Klook or Agoda. In Singapore, the practical answer to "top cash back" is a much shorter list, and the ranking depends less on headline percentages than on three boring things: what form the reward takes, whether it expires, and how much of it you can actually get out.

That's what this guide works through, using ShopBack as the reference point because it's the one most Singapore shoppers end up on — and the one where the rules around tracking, caps and withdrawal decide whether your cashback is real money or a number that never clears.

The expiry column is the part most round-ups skip, and it's where people quietly lose money. A GrabCoins or Shopee Coins balance that sits unspent becomes nothing; ShopBack cashback that sits unclaimed waits for you. That asymmetry is the real reason ShopBack tends to win the "top cash back" question for online shopping, even when another app shows a flashier earn rate.

ShopBack's own model is straightforward: it earns an affiliate commission from a merchant for sending you there, then hands part of that commission back. You pay the retailer's normal price. The cashback comes out of the merchant's marketing budget, not on top of your bill.

## The single habit that determines whether you get paid

Every failed cashback story traces back to the same mistake: not starting the purchase from the cashback platform.

The flow has to be: open ShopBack (app, website, or browser extension) → find the store → click through → complete checkout in that same session. Skip the click-through and there's no tracked session, no matter how many times you've bought from that shop before. A few things break tracking even when you do click through:

- Ad blockers, which strip the affiliate cookie
- Cart items you added *before* clicking through from ShopBack
- Switching browsers, or jumping from a browser click-through into the retailer's app mid-purchase
- Coupon codes from other aggregator sites, which can overwrite ShopBack's tracking with their own tag
- PayLater purchases (excluded from referral requirements since March 2024)

The browser extension removes most of the remembering for desktop shopping. Install it, and the icon flags when you land on an eligible store; activate it and tracking switches on without you having to route through the site first.

## Where the cashback is actually worth chasing

Categories are not equal, and the difference comes down to merchant margins. Travel platforms book inventory rather than hold it, so there's more room in the affiliate budget, which is why travel rates look nothing like grocery rates.

Travel rates change monthly and jump during campaigns. Round-ups checked in September 2026 listed Klook at up to 25–26% during upsized windows, KKday at 15–16.5%, Trip.com at 16–17%, Airalo eSIMs at around 13%, and Agoda at 6.5–7%. Those "upsized" figures are campaign rates, not the everyday number — a family holiday booking a week in Tokyo across accommodation, theme park tickets, transfers and eSIMs could plausibly clear a couple of hundred dollars in cashback, but only if the booking happens during an upsized window.

For everyday retail, check the merchant page rather than the category. ShopBack's Lazada page, as an example, lists up to 8% cashback across beauty, home appliances, sports and household supplies, but 1% on mobiles and tablets, **capped at $7 per order**. The cap matters more than the percentage on big-ticket electronics — a $1,200 phone earns $7, not 1% of the upside you imagined.

Other things that don't earn at all on that merchant: gift card and voucher purchases, store credit redemptions, utility bill payments, LazTravel transactions, and any purchase where part of the total is paid with a non-ShopBack voucher. Fees, taxes and delivery charges are excluded too. If a purchase is fully paid with vouchers and your card balance is $0, cashback is zero.

## Sometimes a higher cashback rate loses to a cheaper price

This is the part most "best cashback app" articles won't tell you, and it's worth internalising before you route everything through a cashback link.

One published price comparison of the same Klook attraction ticket found the direct booking at **SGD84.20** against **SGD86.75** through ShopBack. At the 1% rate that ticket carried (2% with Plus), the cashback came to about 87 cents — roughly S$1.74 with Plus — against an immediate S$2.55 saving from booking direct. The cashback link was the more expensive route.

Cashback is a rebate on top of a price, not a discount on it. If the click-through price is inflated relative to booking direct, no realistic cashback rate fixes that. The habit that protects you here: compare the net price. Look at the direct price and the click-through price side by side, then apply the cashback rate you'd actually earn on that specific merchant, not the headline rate on the app's landing page.

## Getting the money out

Withdrawals are more gated than the marketing suggests, and knowing the thresholds upfront avoids disappointment.

- **S$10 of Confirmed Cashback** is the minimum before you can withdraw anything
- Withdrawals go by **bank transfer to a Singapore-issued bank account** — DBS PayLah! stopped being supported in December 2023
- Bank details need email verification first, and that link expires in about 30 minutes
- Processing typically takes up to **10 calendar days**, and there's a **S$300 daily withdrawal cap**, so a large balance comes out over several days

Pending cashback isn't lost cashback — it's the merchant's return window doing its thing. On Lazada, for instance, ShopBack's own merchant page shows cashback tracked within 3 days and confirmed at around 75 days. Travel is slower still: a hotel booked now won't confirm until after the stay happens. If you're looking at a Pending balance and wondering where your money is, this is usually the answer.

## Stacking, and the offers that aren't shopping

Cashback sits on top of other layers rather than replacing them. Sale price + ShopBack cashback + credit card rewards + any store voucher all come from different pots, and they generally don't cancel each other out. Paying with a card that earns 5% on online transactions, on a purchase already routed through ShopBack, means earning twice on the same dollar.

ShopBack also runs non-shopping offers through partner sign-ups. The Moomoo campaign is the biggest documented example: ShopBack users in Singapore who open a moomoo account and make a cumulative deposit of at least S$2,000 (within 30 days of the first deposit) can receive **S$125 cashback from ShopBack**, on top of moomoo's own welcome rewards. The mechanics: the redemption code arrives in the moomoo app's My Rewards section within 14 business days, and you enter it in the ShopBack app under "Earn More" to unlock it. That campaign ran from 15 November 2025 to 30 September 2026, and unclaimed rewards were to be forfeited after 31 December 2026 — so if you're reading this after that window, check the app for whatever has replaced it rather than assuming the terms still stand.

Treat these as a category of their own. A S$125 credit for moving money you were already going to deposit is a different proposition from cashback on a purchase, and the eligibility rules (new moomoo users, deposit timing, nationality restrictions on account opening) are strict enough that reading the terms first is the whole job.

## A quick start that captures most of the value

1. Sign up with a referral link or code during registration — the code can't be applied afterwards
2. Install the browser extension for desktop shopping and keep the app on your phone
3. Pick one or two regular categories to start with: groceries and marketplace orders for frequency, travel for absolute dollars
4. Before any booking over about S$50, compare the direct price and the cashback price, then check the merchant page for caps and exclusions
5. Clear your cart before clicking through on marketplaces like Lazada, and avoid third-party coupon codes that can hijack tracking
6. Let the balance build to S$10 confirmed, then withdraw to your bank account

## Questions that come up a lot

**Is TopCashback available in Singapore?** No. topcashback.sg redirects to the US site, which is built around US retailers and US payouts. For Singapore merchants, the practical options are ShopBack, plus whatever the store's own vouchers offer.

**Do I get a worse price by shopping through ShopBack?** Usually not — the cashback comes from the merchant's commission, not a markup. But it isn't guaranteed, as the Klook comparison above shows. Compare net prices on big bookings.

**Does the cashback balance expire?** No. ShopBack's cashback doesn't expire; the only gate is the S$10 withdrawal minimum. That's the opposite of GrabCoins, Shopee Coins and Kris+ miles, all of which run on clocks.

**How long until cashback is confirmed?** It varies by merchant. Lazada shows tracking within 3 days and confirmation at roughly 75 days. Travel bookings confirm after the trip is completed.

**Is ShopBack actually popular in Singapore, or just well-marketed?** Survey work by YouGov in May 2024 put ShopBack joint third among the most-used customer reward programmes in Singapore overall — behind Yuu Rewards and FairPrice Link Rewards — and first among Gen Z and Millennial respondents. It was also, in that survey, the only cashback platform of its kind in the top tier.

That's the honest version of "top cash back" in Singapore: one platform pays bank-transferable cash that never expires, and the rest pay in currencies with deadlines. The rate on the merchant page decides how much, the click-through decides whether you get any of it, and the net price comparison decides whether the whole exercise was worth it.
